Heart-Disease Model Transportability Audit

A reproducible stress test of whether strong internal performance survives geographic transport—and whether the resulting probabilities remain trustworthy.

01

Central Finding

The most complex classifier was not reliably better inside the development cohort, and apparently good discrimination did not guarantee reliable probabilities after transport.

Five model families were developed only in Cleveland and then applied, without refitting or recalibration, to Hungary, Switzerland and VA Long Beach. The exercise separates model selection from the harder question of whether a frozen prediction pipeline travels.

No dependable complexity gain

Random forest led logistic regression by only 0.004 corrected ROC-AUC; the 95% confidence interval crossed zero.

Transport was site-specific

Hungary retained strong ranking performance, while Switzerland and VA Long Beach showed larger deterioration.

Calibration exposed hidden failure

Swiss calibration intercepts above +2.3 indicated substantial aggregate underprediction despite usable discrimination.

More variables were not safer

The 13-predictor sensitivity analysis improved internal fit but worsened probability error where added measurements were structurally absent.

Cleveland was the sole development environment. The other three cohorts were untouched targets.

03

Internal Reliability

Repeated nested validation did not support a corrected claim that the nonlinear models outperformed logistic regression.

0.869

Logistic ROC-AUC

Primary eight-predictor representation in Cleveland.

0.874

Random-forest ROC-AUC

The highest primary point estimate, but only marginally higher.

≥ 0.964

Holm-adjusted P values

Across all eight co-primary model comparisons.

The design used stratified 5-fold outer cross-validation repeated five times, 4-fold inner tuning, shared stored splits and participant-cluster bootstrap uncertainty. The corrected random-forest difference versus logistic regression was +0.004 (95% CI −0.012 to 0.021).

04

Frozen Transport

Performance changed by destination, so a single pooled external score would have hidden the operational story.

Hungary

ROC-AUC remained 0.857–0.888 across the five model families.

Switzerland

ROC-AUC fell to 0.747–0.786, with marked aggregate underprediction.

VA Long Beach

ROC-AUC fell further to 0.686–0.743.

Interpretation

Model identity mattered less than the target environment and its measurement process.

05

Calibration Failure

A model may still rank patients reasonably while assigning probabilities that are systematically wrong.

Swiss calibration intercepts ranged from +2.37 to +2.58. This is not a cosmetic metric: it means predicted risks were substantially too low in aggregate for that cohort.

06

Measurement Shift

The data-collection process changed across sites, including which variables were measured at all.

The primary model was deliberately restricted to eight predictors shared across the four cohorts. A conventional 13-predictor sensitivity analysis improved internal logistic-regression ROC-AUC and Brier score from 0.869/0.145 to 0.909/0.120, yet worsened Brier scores for every model in Switzerland and VA Long Beach.

07

Explanation and Capacity

Neither feature explanations nor neural-capacity conclusions were stable enough to be promoted as universal findings.

These analyses turn two common portfolio claims into testable questions. Permutation importance remained model-conditional rather than causal, while larger networks earned no general transportability claim simply by fitting Cleveland better.

09

Limits and Use

This is a methodological audit—not a contemporary clinical validation or deployable diagnostic system.

Historical, selected cohorts

The samples are small, clinically selected and do not represent present-day screening populations.

Prevalent disease endpoint

The target is angiographic disease status, not future cardiovascular risk.

No clinical threshold

Exploratory decision curves do not establish a safe operating point or clinical utility.

No causal claims

Permutation importance describes predictive behaviour, not biological causation.
